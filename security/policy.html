Security

Vulnerability Disclosure Policy

Last updated: 26 July 2026 · Applies to Brooklyn Helpdesk, Inc. and BKHD Engineering

Our commitment

Brooklyn Helpdesk values the work of security researchers and the broader community in keeping our systems and our clients safe. If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in one of our web properties, we want to hear from you and will work with you to understand and resolve it quickly.

How to report

Email security@bkhd.nyc. Our machine-readable contact details are published at /.well-known/security.txt in accordance with RFC 9116.

Please include, where possible:

  • A clear description of the issue and its potential impact.
  • The affected URL, endpoint, or component.
  • Steps to reproduce, proof-of-concept, or relevant request/response details.
  • Any suggested remediation, if you have one.

Scope

This policy covers the public web properties we operate, including:

  • bkhd.nyc and its subdomains (for example blog.bkhd.nyc).

The following are out of scope, and testing against them is not authorized:

  • Systems, networks, devices, or data belonging to our clients.
  • Third-party services we use but do not operate (report those to the relevant vendor).
  • Physical security testing, and testing of any premises or on-site equipment.

Testing guidelines

To keep testing safe and lawful, we ask that you:

  • Act in good faith, avoid privacy violations, and only interact with accounts you own or have explicit permission to use.
  • Do not access, modify, or destroy data that is not yours, and stop at the point where you can demonstrate a vulnerability.
  • Do not run denial-of-service, high-volume automated, brute-force, spam, or social-engineering attacks.
  • Do not publicly disclose the issue until we have had a reasonable opportunity to remediate it.
  • Give us reasonable time to respond and fix the issue before any disclosure.

Safe harbor

We will not pursue or support legal action against researchers who discover and report security vulnerabilities in good faith and in accordance with this policy. We consider activity conducted consistently with this policy to be authorized, and we will work with you to understand and resolve the issue promptly. If legal action is initiated by a third party against you for activity that complied with this policy, we will make it known that your actions were authorized.

Our response

  • We aim to acknowledge your report within 3 business days.
  • We will provide an assessment and expected timeline where practical, and keep you informed of progress.
  • We are happy to credit researchers who wish to be acknowledged once an issue is resolved.

Out-of-scope findings

Reports limited to the following, without a demonstrated security impact, are generally not accepted:

  • Missing best-practice HTTP headers or cookie flags with no proven exploit.
  • Rate-limiting or brute-force findings on non-authentication endpoints.
  • Clickjacking on pages with no sensitive state-changing actions.
  • Self-XSS, or issues requiring an already-compromised device or unlikely user interaction.
  • Reports produced solely by automated scanners without validation.

Preferred languages

We prefer reports in English.
